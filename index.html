Malaysia Public Transport: Demand, Movement, Last-Mile Gaps

An interactive visualisation of population concentration, public transport ridership, and local access infrastructure.

Where People Gather

Chart 1: Population by State

Aggregates district populations to show which states contain the largest population concentration.

Synthesised Insight

Malaysia’s population is concentrated in a small number of states and major districts rather than being evenly distributed.

The state-level bar chart provides the broad demand context, while the ranked district plot shows which local districts contribute most strongly to population concentration.

Why this matters:
Concentrated population areas are more likely to create strong public transport demand, especially around dense urban corridors such as Selangor, Kuala Lumpur, and Putrajaya.

Chart 2: District Population Rank-Size Plot

Ranking districts by population reveals how sharply population concentration drops after the largest urban districts.

From Population to Movement

Population concentration does not automatically translate into transport usage. Ridership trends reveal how movement changes over time and which services carry demand.

Chart 3: National Ridership Trends by Transport Mode

Daily ridership is grouped into broad transport modes to compare national movement trends more clearly.

Chart 4: KTMB Ridership Distribution by Service

Boxplots compare typical daily ridership and variability across KTMB services without repeating similar time-series panels.

Chart 5: KTMB Monthly Ridership Pattern

Monthly ridership is normalised against each service's yearly average to reveal seasonal demand patterns across KTMB services.

The Cost of Movement

Passenger movement is not only about ridership. Accessibility also depends on fare, travel time and the number of transfers required to complete a journey.

Chart 8: Fare and Travel Time by RapidKL Journey

Station-pair fares are compared with estimated travel time, with colour separating direct and transfer journeys.

Chart 9: Transfer Penalty on Travel Time

Median and interquartile ranges show how transfers increase typical travel time and journey variability.

The Last-Mile Question

Public transport access does not end at major rail corridors. This case study looks at Cyberjaya’s bus stop locations to understand how local access points are distributed.

Chart 10: Cyberjaya Bus Stop Locations and POI Access

Bus stop coordinates are plotted over a wider Cyberjaya road basemap. Colour separates stops with a listed nearby point of interest from stops without a listed POI.
