Malaysia Public Transport: Demand, Movement, Last-Mile Gaps

An interactive visualisation of population concentration, public transport ridership, and local access infrastructure.

Where People Gather

Before analysing transport demand, it is useful to understand where people are located. This section compares Malaysian districts by population, highlighting the most populated districts and their states.

Chart 1: Population Heatmap

Uses colour luminance to identify districts with higher population.

Synthesised Insight

Malaysia’s population is concentrated in a limited number of districts rather than being evenly spread.

The heatmap reveals where high-population districts appear across states, while the dot plot makes the ranking and population gap easier to compare.

Why this matters:
These districts may signal stronger public transport demand, setting up the ridership analysis that follows.

Chart 2: Ranked District Dot Plot

Position on the x-axis enables clearer ranking comparison between districts.

From Population to Movement

Population concentration does not automatically translate into transport usage. Ridership trends reveal how movement changes over time and which services carry demand.

Chart 3: National Ridership Stacked Area

The stacked area chart shows how different services contribute to total public transport ridership over time.

Chart 4: KTMB Small Multiples

Separate ridership trends for each KTMB service avoid overlapping lines and support clearer comparison.

Chart 5: KTMB Calendar Heatmap

Daily colour intensity reveals repeated temporal patterns such as weekday peaks or unusually low ridership periods.

The Cost of Movement

Passenger movement is not only about ridership. Accessibility also depends on fare, travel time and the number of transfers required to complete a journey.

Chart 8: Fare-Time Bubble Scatterplot

Each point represents a journey. Longer and more expensive trips become easier to identify, while colour reveals transfer burden.

Chart 9: Transfer Penalty Boxplot

Distribution comparisons reveal whether journeys with more transfers generally involve higher travel time or fare burden.

The Last-Mile Question

Public transport access does not end at major rail corridors. This case study looks at Cyberjaya’s bus stop locations to understand how local access points are distributed.

Chart 10: Cyberjaya Bus Stop Proportional Symbol Map

Each point represents a Cyberjaya bus stop. Larger points indicate stops with a listed point of interest, while tooltips provide street, address, and nearby landmark details.
